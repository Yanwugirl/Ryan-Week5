What Is It Made Of?

One movement. Many sounds. Seen alone, or one after another.

In Week 5 I made a short film: one falling circle shown four times, each time with a different sound. Watching them in a row, each version was heard against the one before it. This page separates them. Watch each version alone, or in a row, and say what you think the circle is made of each time. The circle never changes; only the sound, and the company it keeps.

    The versions

    The sound–image link

    Two ideas from Michel Chion's Audio-Vision helped me think about these versions, alongside Miguel Mera's lecture on sound and image. The first is synchresis: when a sound and an image happen at the same moment, we weld them into a single event, even when the sound was made by something else entirely. A sine tone and a drawn circle become one falling object. The second is added value: a sound gives an image qualities the image does not show, such as weight, hardness or material, and we experience those qualities as if they came from the image itself.

    Each version tests one of these ideas. The dry knock, the ring, the thud and the glass test added value: the same circle seems hollow, metallic, heavy or fragile. The late ring and the early knock test synchresis: how far a sound can drift from the moment of contact before it stops belonging to the circle and becomes an echo, a mistake, or a second event.

    Alone or together

    In the original film the order did part of the work. The silent version came first and made me imagine a sound. The ring came before the late ring, so when the late ring arrived I already expected a sound at the moment of contact, and the delay could feel like an echo. Seen alone, the same delay has nothing to be compared with. It might read as a mistake, or not be noticed at all.

    This is the point Ryan raised: four versions in a row create an impression that the same versions alone, or in other groupings, would not necessarily create. A sequence is not a neutral container. Each version becomes the context for the next one, through expectation and memory. This page makes that difference something you can test on yourself. The Your answers tab puts your answer for each version alone beside your answer in a row, so any change caused by the grouping becomes visible.

    There is a limit to this test. Once you have heard a version, you cannot hear it for the first time again, so whichever way you watch first will shape the second. In a group, the fairest test would give some people the versions alone and others in a row, and compare.
